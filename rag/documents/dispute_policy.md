# Banking Policy: Regulation E & Electronic Fund Transfer Dispute Policy

## Section 1: Customer Liability and Timelines
Under Federal Reserve Regulation E (12 CFR Part 1005):
- **Immediate Notice (Within 2 Business Days)**: Maximum customer liability is $50 if unauthorized access or card theft is reported within 2 business days of learning about the loss.
- **Notice After 2 Business Days (Up to 60 Calendar Days)**: Customer liability is capped at $500 if reported after 2 business days but within 60 calendar days of the periodic statement showing unauthorized activity.
- **Notice After 60 Calendar Days**: Customer may be held liable for all losses occurring after the 60-day window until notice is provided to the financial institution.

## Section 2: Provisional Credit Requirements
- If an investigation cannot be completed within **10 business days** of receiving notice of an unauthorized electronic fund transfer, the bank MUST issue **provisional credit** to the consumer's account for the full disputed amount (plus any charged overdraft fees or interest).
- The bank must provide written notice of provisional credit within **2 business days** of posting.
- For new accounts (opened within 30 days) or point-of-sale/foreign transactions, the investigation window extends to **20 business days** for provisional credit and **90 calendar days** for full resolution.

## Section 3: Fee and Interest Protection
- Any overdraft fees, nonsufficient funds (NSF) fees, or finance charges directly resulting from the disputed transaction must be automatically reversed upon claim verification.
- Scheduled recurring debits (such as auto-loans, mortgages, or utilities) affected by the depleted balance must not incur negative credit reporting while under active investigation.
